02 WHAT WE LOOK FOR

Small clues. Big difference.

Phishing can be convincing. These are some of the signals worth a second look.

↗

Unusual links

Unexpected URLs, shortened links, or web addresses that don’t match the claimed organization.

⌛

Pressure tactics

Urgent deadlines, threats, or warnings designed to rush you into acting without checking.

◇

Sensitive requests

Messages asking you to share a password, one-time code, payment, or personal details.

⊙

Sender mismatch

Display names and email addresses that don’t line up, or addresses from unfamiliar domains.

! A red flag doesn’t prove a message is malicious—and no red flags doesn’t guarantee it’s safe.

03 KNOW THE SIGNS

What is phishing?

Phishing is a type of scam where someone impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick you into revealing information, sending money, or taking an unsafe action.

It can arrive by email, text, phone call, social media, or a fake website. Messages often imitate familiar brands and use urgency to make you act before you think.

⌁COMMON PHISHING TECHNIQUES
01
Impersonation

Pretending to be your bank, employer, delivery service, or someone you know.

02
Urgency & fear

Claiming your account will be locked or a payment is overdue unless you act now.

03
Fake sign-in pages

Sending you to a look-alike page to capture your login details or verification code.

04
Unexpected files

Encouraging you to open an attachment or download something you weren’t expecting.

04 YOUR SAFETY CHECKLIST

Make the safe move.

A few seconds of checking can save you a lot of trouble.

✓

Stop before you click. Be extra cautious when a message is unexpected or alarming.

✓

Verify another way. Contact the person or organization using a number or website you already trust.

✓

Keep secrets private. Never share passwords or one-time codes in response to an unsolicited message.

✓

Report and delete. Use your email or workplace reporting process for suspicious messages.
